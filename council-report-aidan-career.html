Chairman's Verdict

The route is genuinely feasible. Not guaranteed, not easy — but the raw ingredients are better than average for someone 22 years old. The domain overlap between FD3 exterior plastics/glass/mechanisms and consumer hardware is real and specific. The risk isn't capability. The risk is complacency.

The First Principles reframe that emerged is the most strategically important insight from this session: the optimal path is Honda → EV startup (Rivian/Lucid) → Apple, not a direct jump. The Outsider flagged something every other advisor missed: the Design Thinking minor is currently invisible in the portfolio. Conference travel, prototype materials, a 3D printer — all funded. The question is whether that slack gets used deliberately or absorbed by lifestyle creep.

Advisor Responses
Contrarian The Comfort Trap Is the Default Outcome

The comfort trap isn't a risk — it's the default outcome. Honda is a fortress of incremental progression: annual reviews, clear promotion ladders, colleagues who've been there 15 years, a manager who likes you. The path of least resistance is Honda year 3, Honda year 5, Honda year 8. The 3–4 year window closes quietly, not dramatically.

The Apple gap is more brutal than it sounds. Apple Hardware MDE roles get hundreds of applications from people with Stanford/CMU/MIT ME degrees and 2–3 years of consumer product experience at companies like Logitech, Bose, or Snap. Aidan's Honda exterior plastics experience is genuinely relevant, but the competition isn't other auto MEs — it's product engineers who've already made the transition. He'd be competing against people one step ahead on the exact path he's trying to start.

The self-directed project gap is the real problem. Summer 2026 is 6–8 weeks — not enough time to build a portfolio piece that moves the needle at Apple. And starting a new job takes real energy — the first 6 months will consume most creative bandwidth. The window is narrower than it appears. The 3–4 year timeline is optimistic unless he treats portfolio building as a second job starting day one.

First Principles You're Asking the Wrong Question

Strip this back: what does Aidan actually want? He wants to keep making new things for the rest of his career, at a company where the work is excellent and the product matters to people. That's the real goal. Apple and West Coast are the current labels he's attached to it.

That reframe matters because it opens up the strategy. The question isn't "how do I get to Apple in 3–4 years?" It's "what's the fastest path to excellent new product development at a company that takes quality seriously?"

Rivian and Lucid are actually better first moves than Apple for that goal. The domain translation is nearly zero. The culture is product-obsessed. He'd be doing genuine R&D on new vehicles. And critically, landing at Rivian or Lucid at year 2–3 then opens Apple as a subsequent move with actual consumer product company experience on his resume. The ladder is Honda → EV startup → Apple, not Honda → Apple. Trying to skip the middle rung is possible but harder and riskier.

Expansionist Everyone's Thinking Too Small

The FD3 exterior plastics + glass + motion mechanisms combination is genuinely rare for someone 22, and CATIA V5 surfacing at that age is a real differentiator. That's not a "decent foundation" — that's a head start most people don't get.

The West Coast hardware scene is in a talent crunch. Apple, Google, Meta Reality Labs, and the EV companies are all competing for the same pool of mechanism and enclosure engineers. Someone who can bridge automotive manufacturing rigor and consumer product thinking is exactly the hire they can't easily find.

The bigger opportunity is positioning early and loudly. If Aidan builds 2–3 strong self-directed projects in the next 18 months and shows up at SAE and RAPID conferences as someone who thinks across domains, he becomes a known quantity before he even applies. The UV Food Storage capstone is the seed of a consumer product portfolio — finishing it properly could be the most valuable thing he does before July.

Outsider The Design Thinking Minor Is Invisible

Looking at this from the outside: a 22-year-old with two automotive co-ops, a job at Honda, and a portfolio of stamped brackets and roller redesigns wants to work at Apple in 3 years. The honest question is: why would Apple hire this person over someone already in consumer electronics?

The answer has to be "because of something specific I can do that they can't easily find." That thing is CATIA V5 surfacing + automotive-grade DFM rigor applied to consumer product scale. That's a real answer, but it's invisible in the current portfolio and narrative.

The Design Thinking minor is mentioned once and never referenced again. If someone has a Design Thinking minor and their portfolio has zero evidence of it, the honest read is that the minor was a box to check. Apple will notice that immediately. The UV Food Storage module is close — it's the only project with a human at the center — but it's incomplete and framed as a school project, not a product. That needs to change.

Executor Stop Planning. Start Executing.

Stop planning and start executing. Before July 13: finish the UV Food Storage capstone properly. Photograph it like a product. Write a case study leading with the user problem, not the engineering constraints. That's one portfolio piece done before the job starts.

Months 1–6 at Honda: do exceptional work and learn CATIA surfacing on actual exterior components. That's the differentiating skill. Don't start networking yet — get grounded first. Month 6–12: one personal project on nights and weekends. Small mechanism. Consumer scale. Tight tolerances. Something you can hold.

Month 12: first SAE World Congress with intention — two target companies, three target engineers, follow up within 48 hours every time. Month 18–24: LinkedIn presence showing cross-domain thinking, two portfolio pieces live, start conversations at Rivian and Lucid. Month 30–36: apply. The resume by then tells a coherent story.

The one thing that kills this plan: waiting until "things settle down" at Honda before starting personal work. They never settle down. Start before July.
